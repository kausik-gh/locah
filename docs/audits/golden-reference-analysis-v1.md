# Golden reference analysis · v1

Five uploads from the founder (2026-09-29): four full-page captures of earlier LOCAH live
generations (Nalla Veedu Kitchen, Grit Barbell Club, Aranya Homes, Ishant Proteins) and a
contact sheet of twelve other earlier sites. Captures are ~450–575 px wide, so this reads
page-level decisions, not pixel detail. These are a **quality bar**, not templates.

### E. Contact sheet — 12 other earlier sites (the counter-example)

Dental, wedding photographer, industrial pumps, pet grooming, interiors, tuition, jewellery,
courier, car detailing, accountant, florist, caterer. **All share one composition**: split hero
with a photo on the right, the same heavy extended display face, pale teal ground, category cards
showing **giant initial letters** ("G R B T"), the same story split, the same dark CTA band.
They have images and are still generic. Lesson: pictures are necessary but not sufficient —
type, palette world, catalogue treatment and hero composition must change with the business.

## 2. What the three strongest samples share

1. **Every image-bearing slot is filled** (hero, catalogue, story, CTA band) with pictures from
   *one visual world* (same light, surfaces, palette).
2. **The headline is designed**: the product or promise, with a coloured/italic second voice —
   never just the business name.
3. **The catalogue is the centrepiece** and presented as that trade browses: photo menu +
   price list; priced plan cards; alternating cut boards; project cards with starting price.
4. **A story with the owner's own line** as a pull quote, next to a place picture.
5. **Photo CTA band** that repeats the primary action.
6. **One accent colour**, used consistently for price, eyebrows, buttons, rules.
7. **Consistent small craft**: eyebrow pills with a dot, fact chips, rules above titles,
   filter chips, per-card actions, floating WhatsApp.

Their section *order* is actually similar (hero → catalogue → how it works → story → CTA →
contact). Their difference is art direction, not skeleton. Structure should still vary where the
business needs it (plans for a gym, RFQ/applications for B2B, site visit for property), but
restoring imagery, type and catalogue treatment matters most.

## 4. What Phase 2 must restore — and exceed

- Images by default, planned before the design is chosen, in one visual world per site.
- Image-led heroes whenever a picture will exist.
- Featured item pictures for menus/catalogues (TYPE B), never for named projects (TYPE A).
- Story from the owner's own words even without the model.
- A designed headline even without the model (product-led, with an accent).
- Business-specific structure where it matters (plans, projects + site visit, RFQ/applications).
- Avoid the contact-sheet failure: no initial-letter tiles when pictures can exist; type and
  palette world tied to the business.
