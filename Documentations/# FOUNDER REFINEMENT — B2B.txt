2. Three ways a business buys from a supplier
There should be three purchasing modes.
Standing / recurring order handles predictable supply, such as “20 L milk every morning,” “50 kg chicken every day,” or “bread every Monday/Wednesday/Friday.” The source already defines standing orders such as daily milk to a café as recurring chits.    LOCAH — Business Capability Uni…
The owner sets quantity, cadence, cutoff, supplier and effective dates. LOCAH creates the next draft automatically. The owner can edit one occurrence without changing the recurrence, pause it, skip one delivery, or change the future schedule.
Ad-hoc purchase is a manually created requirement: “send me 12 boxes tomorrow.”
Demand-driven purchase is the powerful one: LOCAH calculates what the Business actually needs from real downstream demand and proposes the supplier order automatically.

# FOUNDER REFINEMENT — B2B PROCUREMENT + CHITBRIDGE NETWORK

Lock this product behavior into the P4 Procurement / Trade Network implementation.

A LOCAH Business may be both BUYER and SUPPLIER.

Do not model B2B as merely a supplier-address-book or manual PO screen.

The intended network is:

DOWNSTREAM DEMAND
→ aggregate requirements
→ stock/BOM/yield calculation
→ shortage
→ suggested requisition
→ owner review/approval
→ supplier PO
→ ChitBridge/off-network supplier
→ supplier receives it as incoming B2B sales demand
→ supplier performs the same requirement calculation against its own stock
→ supplier may generate an upstream purchase requirement.

Example:

100 different buyers together order 100 units from a distributor.

Distributor has 70 genuinely usable/uncommitted units.

No other inbound stock and no safety-stock requirement:

Net shortage = 30.

LOCAH should create a suggested procurement requirement for 30,
subject to pack size / MOQ / supplier rules.

DO NOT SEND AUTOMATICALLY by default.

Show:
- total confirmed demand
- breakdown by demand source
- on hand
- reserved
- usable/available-to-promise
- confirmed inbound
- safety stock
- forecast separately
- resulting shortage
- rounding/MOQ effect
- recommended supplier(s)
- expected price/lead time where known

Then:
REVIEW → EDIT → APPROVE & SEND.

If owner has configured a permitted auto-approval threshold, deterministic rules
may approve within that threshold. AI may never raise the threshold.

Support THREE procurement origins:

1. standing / recurring supplier orders
2. manual/ad-hoc purchases
3. demand-driven calculated purchases

Recurring example:
50 L milk from Supplier X every morning.
Allow edit-one-occurrence, skip, pause, resume, or change future recurrence.

Supplier relationship should support the source-defined Procurement/Trade Network
